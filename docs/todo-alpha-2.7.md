## Phase A: Design and Scope (Complete)

### Resolved Decisions

- Loot composition: nested groups flatten into one combined weighted pool; a roll picks one item from it.
- Duplicate items across composed groups: later entries override earlier ones; explicit overrides always win.
- `minAttributes`/`maxAttributes`: a per-attribute score range. Every attribute applicable to the item type is always present; item level moves scores toward the max.
- Enemy spawns: each spawn slot picks exactly one enemy type, with the danger-scaled chance used as a relative weight. Spawn counts stay governed by Walker/materialization caps.
- Loot refresh: looted spots stay empty while the player is in the cell. After 5 minutes away, the cell re-rolls all spots on its next load. Timers are keyed by profile + logical cell and persisted in SQLite.
- Inventory: carried vs saved split from the first slice. Loot enters carried inventory and becomes saved on reaching a den; death drops carried items. Body recovery is a later phase.
- Item level: base = `clamp(playerLevel, minLevel, maxLevel)`; danger adds `round(danger * 5)` plus a random 0..1 bonus levels, capped at `maxLevel`. Low danger never lifts an item above the player's level.
- Attribute scores are always higher = better. Each SWEP/armour maps scores to real values (e.g. Reload Speed shortens reload time).
- Mastercraft: 5% default for weapons/armour, controlled only by loot entries (`mastercraftChance` or `mastercraft: true`). Item definitions cannot force it; the prototype's `mastercrafted` item field is dropped.
- Bosses: cooldowns and active-boss state are in-memory only and reset on server restart.
- Declining loot: the spot stays available and offers the same already-rolled item again; it is never re-rolled until the cell's next refresh.

- `entity_loot` rules use a named `activationChance` (0..1) instead of the prototype's positional `[[groups], 80]` pair.
- Job role: `Job` in persisted player data, defaulting to `Civilian`, exposed through `ply:GetJobRole()`; job selection UI is out of scope for this slice.

### Findings From Phase B Design

- `prop_static` props are compiled into the BSP and are not runtime entities, so they cannot be highlighted or looted. Many template containers (dumpsters, trash cans, trucks) are static. `entity_loot` rejects `prop_static`; Phase G must either switch lootable template props to `prop_physics_override`/`prop_dynamic_override` or add BSP static-prop parsing.
- `statRequirements` must use existing player attribute names. The prototype's `Medical` is `Medicine` in the player schema.
- Loot and enemy `lootGroup` values reference named groups only; the prototype's inline custom-group objects are not supported. Define reusable groups in `loot.json` and use `overrides` there.
- A cell has several environment tags. How multiple matching spawn groups combine is decided in Phase F; Phase B only resolves the matching group ids (or `defaultGroup`).

  - Define stat units and directions per attribute. “More firing speed,” “reload speed,” “range,” and “mobility” can mean either a larger numeric value or a shorter time/lower penalty; no generic scale can safely apply to all weapon classes.

Do not silently persist a new permanent inventory model.
  - Keep multiplayer authority server-side. Clients may request interactions, but must not choose loot, item attributes, boss state, currency, or inventory outcomes.
  - Treat workshop item/class registration as a later extension point with validation and trust boundaries; do not execute arbitrary addon-supplied static data or Lua as part of JSON loading.
  - Scope the initial vertical slice to the requested test content: `itemBandage`, `weaponMeleeCrowbar` / `weapon_zn_melee_crowbar`, and `weaponHandgun9mm` / `weapon_zn_handgun_9mm`. Armor, clothing, crafting, market services, currency, and broad content catalogs remain separate follow-up scope unless promoted here.

## Detour Before Phase E: Locked Top-Down Camera (Done)

- Requested to make aiming easier and closer to Dead Frontier. `cl_thirdpersoncamera.lua` now starts in a locked top-down camera: fixed north-up yaw (world +Y at the top of the screen), pitch blending from 75° (zoomed in) to 89° (zoomed out; 89° rather than 90° avoids the straight-down view flipping). Mouse movement drives a virtual aim cursor (clamped 24 px inside the screen, drawn as a white cross by `cl_crosshair.lua`); the player faces the point under the cursor on the plane through their centre of mass, and WASD is screen-relative (W = up the screen).

- Mouse sensitivity (0.1-2.0, default 0.5, saved in the `zombiesim_mouse_sensitivity` cookie) scales the locked aim cursor and the free-camera look speed; at the default the free camera feels as it did before. It is set with the new "Mouse sensitivity" slider in the Options menu.

- The compass jittered because it read the raw cursor-driven facing. `cl_hud.lua` now draws a smoothed heading (frame-rate-independent easing, rate 10, along the shortest turn so crossing north never spins the long way). Aim and weapons still use the instant facing.
- The free camera's closest-zoom "selfie" view (which flipped to face the player and never worked well) is replaced by an over-the-right-shoulder view: over the last 50 units of zoom the camera glides (smoothstep) to 16 units right, 12 back, and 4 up from the eyes, looking along the free-camera angles, and upward look is allowed to -45° in that range while downward look is capped at 30° (both limits blend with the transition, so zooming in eases the view up instead of snapping). ## Phase D: Item Scaling and Weapon Vertical Slice (Implemented; hands-on weapon feel check pending)

- `gamemode/sv_item_generation.lua` (`ZM_ItemGeneration`): seeded Park-Miller RNG (a seed always repeats the same item), level roll, attribute roll, `CreateInstance`. Level = `clamp(playerLevel, minLevel, maxLevel)` + `round(danger * 5)` + 1 more with probability `danger`, capped at `maxLevel`; this reproduces the prototype's crowbar examples (level 25: danger 0.2 -> 26-27, 0.5 -> 28-29, 1.0 -> 30).
- Attributes: every attribute for the weapon type is always rolled. Non-mastercraft scores follow level progress with a spread of -25%/+10% of the range and never reach the max; all-matching scores are broken up. Mastercraft scores are max or max - 1; 1% of mastercrafts are "perfect" (every score at max, the only case where all scores match). **Assumption to confirm:** the prototype says mastercrafts are "always the max" but also that matching scores should be very rare; this resolves both.
- `ZM_Items.AttributeEffects` maps each score to a multiplier (Damage 0.6-1.6, Range 0.8-1.3, Crushing 0.5-2.0 knockback, ClipSize 0.75-1.5; Swiftness/FiringSpeed 1.3-0.7 and ReloadSpeed 1.4-0.6 scale durations). Value now scales with level, attributes, and x3 for mastercrafts. Melee weapons do not get ReloadSpeed (the prototype lists it "for all weapons", but melee has no reload).

## Phase E: Loot Composition and Roll Engine (Done)

## Phase F: Enemy Definition and Spawn Integration (Implemented; live Walker-ticket spawn check blocked)

- Walker tickets keep their existing schema. `gamemode/sv_enemies.lua` (`ZM_Enemies`) selects the enemy type after a ticket is reserved, using a seeded rng from the ticket id so the same ticket always picks the same type. `WalkerSim:SpawnTicketZombie` resolves the cell, picks the enemy before placement (no eligible enemy rejects the ticket without creating an entity; the materializer already rejects failed placements), creates `enemy.entity`, and applies the definition.
- Selection: every spawn group mapped from the cell's environment tags combines into one pool (a later group replaces an earlier group's weights for the same enemy), falling back to `defaultGroup` when no tag matches. An enemy is eligible when the cell danger is within its inclusive `minDanger`..`maxDanger`, it is not a boss, and its entity class supports the Walker ticket and definition interface (`SetWalkerTicket` + `ApplyEnemyDefinition`). Spawn weight, health, and speed interpolate across each enemy's own danger range; speed multiplies the entity's base walk speed.
- Spawn budget: the existing Walker live cap (`zombiesim_walker_active_cap`) and population target already limit spawns per cell, so no second budget was added.
- `zn_walker_zombie` gained `ApplyEnemyDefinition(enemy, danger)` and uses the scaled walk speed everywhere. On death it resolves its ticket as before and then calls `ZM_Enemies:OnEnemyKilled`, which rewards once per victim (duplicate callbacks, non-player kills, and despawns get nothing): enemy `xp` via `ply:AddXP` (now persisted and networked) and a `ZM_Loot:RollEnemyDrop` roll given straight to the killer's backpack. Phase H will route drops through the loot popup instead.
- New `xp` field on enemy definitions (integer, default 0); the shipped walker gives 10 and the rare walker 25.
- Commands: `zn_spawn_enemy [enemyId|auto] [danger]` (development spawn in front of the player, not Walker-ticketed), `zn_kill_enemies` (kills nearby defined enemies credited to the player), `zn_test_enemies`.
- Verified in-game on preview: 45/45 scripts, 0 static errors, 11/11 static, 23/23 inventory, 11/11 loot, and 10/10 new enemy tests (inclusive danger bounds, per-range weight interpolation, tag group combination and default fallback, boss exclusion, empty pools with a reason, unsupported entity classes skipped, 10,000-pick distribution and ticket repeatability, health/speed scaling on real spawned walkers, one reward per kill with no reward for duplicates or non-player kills, despawn gives no reward, unknown cells rejected with no entity left behind, shipped data selects both walkers). ## Phase G: World Loot Spots and Cell Lifecycle (Implemented; hands-on feel check pending)

- Matching: map-placed props whose runtime class (`prop_physics`/`prop_physics_multiplayer`/`prop_dynamic`; Hammer `*_override` classes spawn as these) and normalized model match an `entity_loot` rule. Spots are identified by `MapCreationID()`, which is stable across loads of the same map; runtime-spawned props are never spots. `prop_static` still cannot be looted (Phase B finding).
- Lifecycle (`gamemode/sv_loot_spots.lua`, `ZM_LootSpots`): initialized from `GM:PlayerSpawn` once the first loaded player is on their own city-cell map (never in dens or safe-zone cells). State lives in SQLite tables `loot_cells` (profile, cellId, generatedAt, lastPresentAt) and `loot_spots` (spotKey, state, rolled item). Presence is recorded every 15 s while players are in the cell and at shutdown; a load 300 s or more after the last presence re-rolls every spot (activation chance per prop), otherwise looted spots stay empty and rolled items are kept. This survives map reloads and server restarts.
- Interaction: pressing E near an active spot (nearest within 110 units, so small props are easy to hit in the top-down view) starts a 2 s search; moving out of range or dying cancels it. The server rolls the item once (cell danger, searcher's level), saves it on the spot, and sends an offer with a single-use token (expires after 120 s). Accept grants the item and marks the spot looted (from up to 200 units); decline keeps the same item on the spot, even across reloads. One player at a time can claim a spot. Default Use (picking up physics props) is blocked on active spots.
- Client (`cl_loot_popup.lua`): yellow tint and a pulsing "?" over active spots, a "Searching..." bar above the player, the prototype's offer window (name in gold with "(MC)" for mastercrafts, level, thumbnail with an attribute/value tooltip and a placeholder until item icons exist, Accept/Decline docked at the bottom; closing the window declines), and short result messages. This covers the loot window part of Phase H.
- Commands: `zn_loot_spots` (matching props and spot states), `zn_loot_spots_refresh` (force a re-roll), `zn_test_loot_spots`; bridge-only `zombiesim_dev_teleport_cell <gridX> <gridY>` for moving the test player through the normal world transition.
- Verified in-game on preview: 48/48 scripts, 11/11 static, 23/23 inventory, 11/11 loot, 10/10 enemy, and 9/9 new loot spot tests (rule matching by runtime class and normalized model, activation and persistence, the 300 s refresh boundary and forced refresh, search -> offer -> accept with token replay rejected, decline keeps the same item across a reload and old tokens are rejected, claims/range/expiry, a failed grant keeps the spot and item, profile and cell isolation, stored spots without an entity are dropped). Live: grid cell (5,6) = cell 149 has 40 matching props (cars and barrels) and loaded 13 spots; two barrels were already searched and looted in the session.
- Pending: hands-on feel of searching, the popup, and highlight readability.
